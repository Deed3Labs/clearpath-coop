Move money — one modal, three destinations

Savings, the yield pool and BurnerBonds all use the same shape: amount, quick amounts, route, keypad, consequence, summary. Two changes here — the bottom metrics now sit in a bordered container rather than floating under a hairline, and bonds join the pattern, which takes one extra control and one honest exception.

One box, not two. Four rows and a divided footer — taken from the bond modal, which already had the right shape. What the choice EARNS is tinted text on its own row; what it ADDS TO THE CREDIT LIMIT is the green footer. Colour does the work a second container was doing.

Every destination has the same five lines: one earn row, three facts, one credit-limit footer. A member who has used one modal can read any of them.

The credit-limit line is green and always last, because it is the one consequence common to all three products — and the one that is easiest to forget you are getting.

03 — BurnerBond, and the one exception

Bonds break one rule and it has to be handled, not hidden. Everywhere else the number you type is the number that moves. With a bond you choose face value — what you get back — and a smaller, discounted amount leaves your account. So the hero carries both: face value large, what you pay stated directly beneath it, never buried in the summary.

One arrow, not two. Same circle, same position, same background as the swap on the other modals — only the glyph differs. Two heads means you can flip it; one head means you cannot, and that reads instantly without changing anything else. A two-headed swap would promise a reversal the product cannot do before maturity.

A padlock was tried first and did not survive. At 14px the arrow detail on the shackle disappeared and it read as a plain lock — which loses the direction, the thing the icon is actually there to show. Locked is already said twice on the screen: Matures Aug 2028 on the leg, and the note under the summary.

Term is the only chip row. Face value is typed on the keypad, so quick amounts would just be guesses at a number the member already has in mind — and unlike savings, there is no habitual round figure for a bond.

Yield is one line, not two: 7.0% fixed · +$632.81. The rate and what it is worth in dollars are the same fact, and a buyer needs both to compare terms — splitting them made the reader do the multiplication.

The To leg carries the maturity date rather than a balance, because a bond has no running balance to show until it exists.

05 — Processing, and done

A spinner inside the button is too quiet for money moving. The modal replaces its own content — the member sees what is happening, that it went through, and that they are free to walk away.

Three named steps, not a bare spinner. They are the three things that actually happen, in order, and a member who has just handed over money can watch it land. If one stalls, the screen already shows which.

“You can close this” is the important sentence. The instinct with money in flight is to sit and stare at the screen. Saying it finishes on its own removes that, and it is true — the transaction is already submitted.

Done shows the same five lines, past tense. Adds to your credit limit becomes your credit limit rose by. Same numbers the member saw before confirming, so nothing arrives as a surprise.

“Save more” sits under Done because the moment right after a deposit is the only moment someone is inclined to make another.

The bond's done state leads with the gain, not the payment. The member already knows what left their account — they confirmed it. What they bought is $632.81 and a date, so that is what the screen says.

“Adding it to your credit line” is a real step, not filler. It is the least obvious thing a bond does and the one most worth watching happen, because it is why a locked position is not a locked-away position.

06 — When it does not go through

Nothing moved

Your $250.00 is still in your cash account

Taken from your cash account

“Nothing moved” is the headline, not the error. The only question a member has when something fails with their money is whether they still have it. Answer that first; the reason is a subline.

The steps stay on screen and show the reversal — taken, then returned. A member who watched money leave needs to watch it come back, not be told it never left.
